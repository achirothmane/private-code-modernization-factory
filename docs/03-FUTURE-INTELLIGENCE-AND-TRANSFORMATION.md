# Future Intelligence, Transformation, and Diagnosis

## A product vision larger than current AI tooling

Software evolution may eventually benefit from intelligence capable of understanding requirements and behavior across languages, architectures, runtime environments and changing platforms.

Such intelligence might propose transformations, infer likely invariant violations, generate test candidates, diagnose failed migrations, compare repairs, or select experiments that reduce uncertainty.

The product's vision can explore these possibilities without redefining ModFactory as a generic AI agent, model router or universal reasoning system.

## Current research provides concrete caution

The [research log](research-log.md) records that evidence and target compatibility filters sometimes removed apparent model tasks before inference was justified. It also records the difference between a large repository and the much smaller context needed for a specific migration.

The [Wave 7H measured ordinary-model benchmarks](../benchmarks/results/wave7h-first-measured-models-2026-09-21.md) produced failed patch-validity results for the examined migration task. A larger 7B parameter model did not automatically make the output an applicable patch; the recorded decision favored decomposing the problem rather than escalating compute.

These are specific findings, not proof that all models will fail or succeed at modernization.

## Evidence before adaptive authority

Future intelligent transformations could become increasingly adaptive, but a system that chooses a patch must not silently rewrite its own independent acceptance criterion.

Possible research areas include counterexample-guided repair, richer static/dynamic analysis, model-generated characterization tests with external validation, executable specifications, uncertainty estimation, cross-language behavioral checks and integration with expert review.

Every claimed improvement needs comparison against robust alternatives and reproducible evaluation.

## New science and AGI/ASI possibilities

Unknown forms of machine intelligence or computing may alter software development. AGI and ASI remain uncertain scenarios with no guaranteed arrival or abilities.

A future model capable of writing fluent patches does not automatically know whether a migration preserves an organization's intended behavior, especially when the intent is undocumented. Even a very advanced system must have justified authority and an adequately independent evidence boundary.

## No compulsory framework

Do not hard-code a permanent model provider, force all tasks through an LLM, mandate long-context compute, or create new infrastructure merely because the future vision is ambitious.

When a coding or research task is actually authorized, select mechanisms based on their observed reliability, compatibility, costs, privacy and verification requirements.

**Intelligence may broaden what changes can be proposed; trustworthy evidence determines what can reasonably be accepted.**
